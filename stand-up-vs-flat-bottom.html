Stand-up Pouch vs Flat Bottom Bag

How the two most common retail pouch formats compare on shelf display, print area, capacity, material use and which product they suit.

For coffee, snacks, nuts, powders and pet food buyers
← Back to Buying Guides

Stand-up pouches and flat bottom bags both stand upright on the shelf, but they are built differently. The choice affects how the pack displays, how much artwork fits, how much it holds and how it stacks in transport. This guide explains the trade-offs so you can pick the format that matches your product and shelf position.

1. Side-by-side comparison

2. When a stand-up pouch is the better fit

The stand-up pouch (doypack) is the general-purpose retail standard. It works well when:

  • You pack snacks, nuts, dried fruit, tea, ground coffee, powders or small treat sizes.
  • You want a cost-efficient pack with zipper, tear notch and optional window.
  • Order quantities and shelf position call for a clean, familiar pouch shape.
  • Your filling line handles pre-made pouches at regular retail weights.

3. When a flat bottom bag is the better fit

The flat bottom or eight-side seal bag is chosen when presentation and presence matter. It works well when:

  • You want a box-like stable base and a strong premium shelf profile.
  • The artwork needs large flat front and side panels.
  • Pack size moves into mid or larger volumes, such as specialty coffee, nuts, protein powder or pet food.
  • Steady stacking on pallets and a neat out-of-home shelf display are required.

4. Shared options for both formats

Either base can be fitted with the same functional accessories, selected by product and filling method:

  • Resealable zipper with a tear notch for clean first opening.
  • Degassing valve for whole-bean coffee packs.
  • Clear or frosted window where the product should be visible.
  • Handle or hang hole where the retail channel needs it.
  • Barrier structures from standard PET/PE to foil-lined PET/ALU/PE and kraft laminates.

Barrier level for coffee is covered in the coffee bag material guide; pet food puncture and barrier needs are covered in the pet food barrier guide.

Common questions

What is the main difference between a stand-up pouch and a flat bottom bag?

A stand-up pouch leans on a bottom gusset between two flexible panels and uses less material. A flat bottom bag forms a square box base with sealed side and bottom panels, stands very steadily, and offers a larger flat print area.

Which bag looks more premium on the shelf?

Flat bottom bags generally present a more premium, box-like profile and bigger artwork panels. Stand-up pouches remain a clean, cost-effective standard for mid-market retail.

Which format holds more product?

The flat bottom's box base usually supports a larger fill volume and stable pallet stacking. The stand-up pouch is efficient for regular retail weights; the flat bottom is typical for mid and larger packs.

Can both formats use a zipper, valve or window?

Yes. Both can take a zipper, tear notch, optional window and, for coffee, a valve. Closure and accessories are chosen by product and filling method, not by base type.
