Fit and Function

How a Spout Pouch Is Specified

The spout, the film and the seal are chosen together, and the filling process decides all three.

Spout and cap choices

  • Spout diameter chosen to match your filling nozzle and flow rate
  • Screw cap for reliable reclosure, flip-top for fast dispensing
  • Center spout for symmetrical packs, corner spout for a controlled pour
  • Pull-ring or tear-open spout where the pack is single use
  • Spout grade matched to cold fill, hot fill or retort sterilization
  • Gusset depth set by fill volume, not by a fixed catalogue size

Products that suit a spout pouch

  • Liquid detergents, cleaners and refills
  • Sauces, pastes and condiments
  • Juices, concentrates and beverage bases
  • Liquid soap, shampoo and personal care refills
  • Ice cream and dairy bases in larger packs
  • Liquid pet supplements and nutritional products
Fill Temperature and Strength

Why the Same Pouch Cannot Do Every Liquid

Hot fill, cold fill and retort sterilization each need a different film and spout grade.

Fill temperature decides the film

A cold-filled detergent and a hot-filled sauce do not use the same structure. Hot fill needs a film and spout that tolerate the temperature at the moment of filling, while a product that is sterilized after sealing needs a retort-grade structure with a suitable spout grade. Tell us the filling temperature and whether the pack is pasteurized or retort sterilized; the structure follows from that.

Viscosity matters as well. A thin liquid needs a smaller spout to control flow, while a thick paste may need a wider opening to fill and dispense at a reasonable speed.

Handling and strength

  • Drop and stack strength — a filled liquid pouch carries weight, so the seal width and film thickness are set for the pack size, not copied from a smaller format
  • Barrier where it counts — foil or metallised structures for oxygen and light sensitive products, standard structures for short shelf life
  • Handle or carry fitment — larger packs can take a handle or a reinforced top panel
  • Print surface — a liquid pack is often read at arm's length, so the front panel carries the brand and the back carries dosage and warnings
Questions

Common Questions About Spout Pouches

What buyers ask before a first liquid packaging order.

Can a spout pouch be retort sterilized?

Yes, when the film and the spout are both retort grade. The sterilization temperature, hold time and pressure of your process determine which grades can be used, so those parameters are needed before a structure is proposed.

What spout size should I choose?

Match the spout to your filling nozzle first, then to how the consumer pours. Thin liquids usually need a smaller opening, thick pastes a wider one. Spout diameter and cap style are fixed at quoting stage.

Is a spout pouch suitable for hot fill?

Hot fill is possible with a structure and spout rated for the fill temperature. The fill temperature, cooling method and product acidity all affect the choice, so they should be stated when requesting a quotation.

Can the pouch stand on the shelf after filling?

Yes. A stand-up spout pouch uses a bottom gusset so the filled pack stands upright for retail display; flat spout pouches are used where the pack sits in a carton or is hung.
