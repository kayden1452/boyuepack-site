Sizes and Strength

Pet Food Bags From Retail Pouch to 25 kg Bulk

The size ladder and the film strength are decided together, because a 25 kg bag and a 1 kg pouch do not fail in the same way.

The pack size ladder

  • 1 kg to 3 kg retail pouches — stand-up with zipper, optional window
  • 4 kg to 8 kg mid packs — flat bottom or eight-side seal with a handle
  • 10 kg to 25 kg bulk bags — heavier film, slider or sewn-in closure
  • Sample and trial packs for new recipes or new markets
  • Treat pouches in small retail sizes with a resealable top
  • Private label runs built around your shelf and pallet dimensions

What the film has to survive

  • Granules and extruded kibble scratching the surface during filling
  • Bones, hard treats and rough textures puncturing a thin film
  • Stacking pressure in the warehouse and during transport
  • Repeated opening and closing over the life of a large bag
  • Humid or warm destination markets where moisture uptake is fast
  • Light exposure where fats would otherwise turn rancid
Film and Print

What the Film Has to Survive, and How the Pack Sells

Puncture resistance protects the product in the warehouse; printing protects it on the shelf.

Puncture resistance is not optional

Pet food is abrasive. Kibble edges and hard treats abrade the inner layer while filling and while the bag is handled in a warehouse. A film that works for a soft snack can fail here, which is why reinforced structures and heavier inner layers are common for chunky or bony products. If pinholes or leaks have shown up in your current pack, tell us — that usually points to a structure change rather than a printing change.

Large packs also carry weight. The seal width and film thickness have to suit the filled mass, and a handle needs a reinforced area so it does not tear out under load.

Printing for a crowded shelf

  • Digital printing — practical for a new brand, several recipes or several pack sizes launched together, because there is no cylinder cost per design
  • Gravure printing — the route once one design repeats in volume and unit cost matters more than flexibility
  • Panel planning — front for brand and product, back for the nutrition table, feeding guide, batch and best-before area
  • Matte or gloss — matte reads as premium and hides fingerprints, gloss gives stronger colour on shelf
  • Window or no window — kibble often sells better when the customer can see the piece size and colour
Questions

Common Questions About Pet Food Bags

The points buyers raise before a pet food packaging order.

Which structure is best for 10 kg or 25 kg pet food bags?

Large packs normally use a reinforced structure with a heavier inner seal layer for puncture resistance, plus a handle and a slider or strong zipper closure. The exact film and thickness are set by the filled weight and how the bags are stacked and shipped.

Can you supply bags for both retail and bulk sizes?

Yes. The same brand can be produced across the size ladder, from 1 kg retail pouches up to 25 kg bulk bags, with printing matched so the range reads as one family on the shelf.

Do pet food bags need a window?

Not always, but a window helps where piece size and colour sell the product. A clear or frosted panel is added during pouch making and does not change the barrier of the rest of the bag.

How do I know if my current pet food bag is under-protected?

Typical signs are kibble going soft or clumping before the printed best-before date, fatty treats turning rancid early, and pinholes or scratches appearing with chunky products. Each points to a different fix: moisture barrier, oxygen barrier or puncture resistance.
