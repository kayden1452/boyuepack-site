Packaging Guide

Which Flexible Pouch Type Should You Choose?

A side-by-side comparison of pouch formats, so you can match the bag to your product state, shelf display, barrier requirement and filling line.

For food, beverage, pet food and supplement packaging buyers
← Back to Buying Guides

Flexible packaging comes in a small set of standard formats, each built around a different product and display situation. The format decides how the bag stands, how it opens, how much print area it offers and what filling line it suits. This guide compares the main BOYUE pouch types and points to the format that usually fits each product.

2. Stand-up pouch (doypack)

A stand-up pouch has a bottom gusset that lets the filled bag stand upright on the shelf. It is the general-purpose retail format for solids, powders and granules, and accepts a resealable zipper, tear notch, optional window, valve and hang hole.

Common uses: snacks, nuts, dried fruit, tea and coffee, small pet treat packs and sample sizes. See the stand-up pouch product page for structures and features.

3. Flat bottom / eight-side seal bag

The flat bottom bag forms a box-like base that stands square and stable on the shelf. Its large flat panels give a premium print area, which suits specialty coffee, mid-size pet food, nuts and protein powder where shelf presentation matters.

It is the usual upgrade from a stand-up pouch when the pack needs a more premium profile or a larger fill volume. Compare the two in our stand-up vs flat bottom guide.

4. Spout pouch

For pourable liquids and pastes, a spout pouch places a pouring nozzle and cap at a center or corner position. The spout gives a controlled pour and a tight reclosure, and the bag can stand on a bottom gusset or lie flat inside a carton.

Common uses: juice and concentrates, sauces and pastes, yogurt, liquid detergents and cleaners, personal care refills and liquid pet supplements.

5. Shaped pouch

A shaped pouch is die-cut to an irregular silhouette around the product concept. It uses the same barrier film families as standard bags; the special outline and panel design are what make it stand out on a crowded shelf. It suits candy, specialty snacks, baby food and pet treats where branding matters.

6. Aluminum foil bag

When the product must be shielded from oxygen, moisture and light, an aluminum foil bag provides an opaque barrier layer. Foil bags are used for dry goods, powders, spices, supplements and long-shelf-life products where a clear panel would expose the contents to light.

7. Retort pouch

A retort pouch is built from heat-resistant layers designed to survive high-temperature sterilization. It is used for shelf-stable ready meals, meat, fish, curry and sauces. The exact structure is set by the sterilization temperature, hold time and product acidity; see the retort structure guide for details.

8. Five steps before you choose a format

  • Product state: solids, powders, granules, liquids or pastes — liquids point to a spout pouch, solids to stand-up or flat bottom.
  • Shelf display: a standard retail shelf uses a stand-up pouch; a premium box-like presence uses a flat bottom bag; a shaped outline is for standout branding.
  • Barrier need: longer shelf life, humid markets and light-sensitive products call for foil or metallized structures; check the barrier and valve guidance for coffee and the pet food barrier guide.
  • Filling line: pre-made pouches suit manual, semi-automatic or rotary filling; confirm bag format and closure against your filling method.
  • Volume and artwork: short runs and multiple designs suit digital printing; large repeat volumes suit gravure. This affects the printing route more than the bag format.

Common questions

Which pouch type works best for snacks and dried food?

Stand-up pouches are the standard. They take a zipper, optional window and hang hole, and work from sample sizes to regular retail packs. Choose a flat bottom bag when you want a box-like premium display.

Which bag should I use for liquids and sauces?

Use a spout pouch for pourable liquids and pastes. Standard stand-up pouches seal closed and are meant for solids, powders and granules.

When do I need a foil bag or a retort pouch?

A foil bag protects dry goods, powders and supplements from oxygen, moisture and light. A retort pouch is a separate sterilizable structure for ready meals and processed foods that go through high-temperature retort processing.

Do shaped pouches protect the product the same way as standard bags?

Yes. The barrier film and closure are chosen by product and shelf life; the shaped silhouette is a display and branding choice on top of that protection.
