Format and Fit

What a Stand-Up Pouch Can and Cannot Do

The format is flexible, but the gusset, closure and film have to match your product and filling line.

What sets the format

  • Bottom gusset width sets how much the pouch holds and how firmly it stands
  • Panel size follows from fill volume, not from a catalogue size list
  • Press-to-close zipper for low cost, slider for heavy repeated opening
  • Tear notch above the zipper for a clean first opening
  • Window in clear or frosted film, shaped or rectangular, when the product should be visible
  • Handle or hang hole where the retail channel needs it

Where stand-up pouches work best

  • Snacks, nuts, dried fruit and confectionery
  • Coffee and tea, with valve and zipper options
  • Powders and granules, with a wide gusset for fill volume
  • Pet treats and small pet food packs
  • Household refills where shelf space is limited
  • Sample and travel sizes for new product launches

Questions

Common Questions About Stand-Up Pouches

The points buyers usually check before a first order.

What is the difference between a stand-up pouch and a doypack?

They are the same format. Doypack is the common trade name for a stand-up pouch with a shaped bottom gusset that lets the filled bag stand upright on the shelf.

Can a stand-up pouch be used for liquids?

For liquids and semi-liquids a spout pouch is usually the better choice because the spout gives a controlled pour and a reliable reclosure. Stand-up pouches are normally used for solids, powders and granules.

Do stand-up pouches need a minimum order per design?

The practical minimum depends on the printing method. Digital printing avoids cylinder cost and makes short runs and multiple designs workable; gravure printing is set up around large repeat volumes.

Can you print different designs on the same order?

Yes, with digital printing each design is printed from its own file, so a launch can start with several SKUs or seasonal variants in one run.
