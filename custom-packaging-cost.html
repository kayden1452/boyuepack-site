What Custom Packaging Really Costs — and What Moves the Price

Two pouches can look identical and still differ several times over in unit cost. This guide explains what a quotation is built from, so you can compare offers like for like and see where the money actually goes.

A custom flexible package is priced as a specification, not as a product. The same bag size can carry a three-layer metallised film or a four-layer aluminium foil laminate, be printed digitally or by gravure, and run as one design or six. Each of those changes the material cost, the setup cost and how much of the setup is spread across the order. That is why a supplier who publishes a single number is either quoting the cheapest possible structure or quoting something that will not match your brief.

How to use this page: read it before you request quotations. If two suppliers return very different prices, the difference is usually one of the six variables below — not a better or worse margin.
The Cost Build-Up

Six Variables That Set the Unit Price

Every quotation is a combination of these. Change one and the number moves.

1. Bag format and size

Material is charged by area. A flat bottom bag uses more film than a stand-up pouch of the same fill weight because of the box base and the extra seal panels; a spout pouch adds a welded spout and cap. Oversizing a bag for a smaller fill weight is the most common silent cost in packaging.

2. Film structure

A three-layer PET/VMPET/PE is a metallised barrier; a PET/AL/PE uses aluminium foil and costs more per square metre but delivers a much higher oxygen and light barrier. Retort grades, nylon-containing structures and paper-laminated kraft all sit at different price points.

3. Total thickness

Thicker film means more resin, more lamination adhesive and slower running on the filling line. Thickness is set by the mechanical demand — puncture resistance for pet food, drop strength for bulk packs — not by habit.

4. Print method

Digital printing carries no cylinder cost, so it suits short runs and multiple designs. Gravure requires an engraved cylinder per colour, which is a one-off cost that only pays back across a larger volume. The crossover depends on quantity and colour count.

5. Quantity and number of designs

Setup, proofing, film minimums and machine time are fixed per run. Ten thousand units of one design is a different price from ten thousand units split across five designs, even though the total material is the same.

6. Accessories and finishing

A one-way degassing valve, a zipper, a slider, a spout and cap, a window, laser scoring or a shaped profile each add material and an extra process step. Matte finish, soft-touch and registered spot colours also change the print cost.

Comparing Print Methods

Where Digital and Gravure Cross Over

Not a price table — a comparison of the cost behaviour behind each method.

One-off setupDigital: artwork and proofing only. Gravure: an engraved cylinder for every colour.
Effect of small quantityDigital absorbs setup across few units, so short runs stay workable. Gravure spreads a large setup across few units, so the unit price is high.
Effect of large quantityGravure unit cost keeps falling as the cylinder cost is amortised. Digital reaches a floor set by run speed and material.
Changing a designDigital: a new file, no new tooling. Gravure: new cylinders per colour, so redesign is a real cost.
Several designs in one orderDigital handles multiple designs in the same run. Gravure multiplies cylinder sets per design.
Best fitDigital for launches, market tests, seasonal variants and short repeat orders. Gravure for established designs with steady high volume.
Reducing Cost

Six Levers That Actually Lower the Unit Price

In order of impact. None of them requires a worse-looking pack.

  • Consolidate designs onto one bag. If two SKUs share a fill weight, one printed design with a shared base and variant coding removes a whole print run.
  • Size the bag to the product, not to the shelf. Removing 10 mm of excess headspace across a large order is material money back on every unit.
  • Use the barrier the shelf life needs. Aluminium foil is not needed for a six-month ambient snack; metallised film often covers it at a lower material cost.
  • Fix the artwork before the run starts. Re-proofing and re-plating after approval is the most avoidable charge in the whole quotation.
  • Plan the annual volume, then order in releases. A committed programme usually earns a better structure of price than the same total split into ad-hoc enquiries.
  • Match the format to your filling line. A format your line cannot run efficiently adds labour cost that never appears on the pouch invoice.

Questions

Common Questions About Packaging Pricing

The points buyers raise most often before comparing quotations.

Why is there no fixed price list on this site?
Because a custom pouch is not a catalogue item. Unit price moves with film structure, thickness, print method, quantity, number of designs and accessories. A published number would either be wrong for your specification or be based on the cheapest possible structure, which is misleading when you compare suppliers.
Is a small trial order much more expensive per unit?
Usually yes, and it is a structural effect rather than a markup. Setup, plate or cylinder cost, film minimums and machine time are spread across fewer units. Digital printing removes the cylinder cost, which is why short runs are quoted on digital rather than gravure.
Does printing more colours cost more?
On digital printing, additional colours do not require new cylinders, so the difference is small. On gravure, each colour needs an engraved cylinder, so the number of colours increases both the one-off cost and the setup work per run.
Do samples cost money?
Printed proofs and physical samples can be arranged once the specification is agreed. Whether a sample is free or charged, and whether the cost is credited against the first order, is confirmed at quotation stage.
Can the price change after a quotation?
A quotation is tied to the specification, quantity and material market at that moment. If artwork, structure, size or quantity change, the price is re-quoted. Film and resin prices also move, so quotations are usually time-limited, and the validity period is stated on the offer.
