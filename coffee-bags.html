Specifying Your Bag

How to Specify a Coffee Bag Without Guessing

Three decisions drive the structure, the cost and the shelf result: pack format, filling method and barrier level.

Start from pack weight and format

  • Flat bottom / eight-side seal — sits square on the shelf, large flat print panel, best for premium 250 g to 1 kg retail packs
  • Stand-up pouch — the cost-efficient retail standard for 250 g to 500 g, accepts valve and zipper
  • Quad seal — tall narrow profile for shelf presence and tight retail spacing

Then match your filling line

  • Pre-made pouches — for manual, semi-automatic or rotary filling; bag is finished and ready to fill
  • Valve or no valve — whole bean packs usually need a one-way valve; ground coffee depends on roast level and how soon it is packed
  • Finish and closure — matte, gloss, kraft look, window, tear notch and tin tie are all specified at quoting stage

Questions

Common Questions About Coffee Bags

Answers to what buyers ask most before placing a first order.

Can I order several coffee designs in one production run?

Yes. Digital printing has no cylinder cost, so multiple designs, seasonal artwork and small runs can be produced together. Gravure printing becomes the better route once one design repeats in large volume.

What coffee bag sizes can you produce?

From small sample packs up to 5 kg bulk bags. Sizes are customised to your pack weight rather than limited to catalogue dimensions.

How do I choose between flat bottom and stand-up for coffee?

Flat bottom bags stand square and give a bigger print area, which suits premium specialty brands. Stand-up pouches use less material per pack and remain the standard for mid-market retail. Both accept a valve and zipper.
